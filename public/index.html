II · Manifesto
Every stitch is built around one hair.
Two to nine strands of horsetail are wrapped in silk and laid into indigo cloth by hand. A four-hundred-year-old craft of the Shui people of Guizhou. Eighty hours per piece. No machines. No shortcuts.
III · The Cloth
III · The Cloth

Before
a single
stitch

The cloth must become midnight before it can hold a thread. Indigo leaves are gathered each summer in the mountain fields of southern Guizhou, fermented in earthenware vats for nine days, set with lime, then brushed onto cotton by hand. Twenty-four dips for the deepest black.

Strobilanthes cusia· Sandu, Guizhou · 9 days · 24 dips
IV · The Thread
IV · The Thread
One needle.
One hair.
One silk.

Two to nine strands of horsetail are bundled, wrapped in silk by spinning between thumb and forefinger, and only then is the needle threaded. The wrapped hair raises the stitch off the cloth — sculptural, almost three-dimensional. There is nothing else like it in textile art.

2–9strands per stitch

VII · The People

A people,
a living
language

The Shui have lived in the mountains of southern Guizhou for over a thousand years — one of China's most ancient and least-known ethnic groups. They have their own written script, one of the few surviving pictographic writing systems in the world. And they have their embroidery — used not as decoration, but as memory.
